# Architecture

```
 home network                                 jhubuntu (docker compose)                  anywhere
┌────────────────────┐   websocket (out)   ┌───────────────────────────────────┐   https   ┌──────────┐
│ ESP32-CAM          │ ──────────────────▶ │ app (node 20)                     │ ◀──────── │ browser  │
│  camera task       │   jpeg + json       │  device-socket   frames ─▶ mjpeg  │           │ phone    │
│  frame hub         │                     │  devices         motion ─▶ events │  socket.io│          │
│  stream / local    │ ◀── http (ota) ──── │  browser-socket  storage          │ ◀───────▶ │          │
└────────────────────┘                     │  mongo: users, devices, events    │           └──────────┘
```

## Firmware (`firmware/`, ESP-IDF v5.1.6, C)

Modules follow ESW0009: one encapsulated task per `KL_*.c` file (the prefix ESW0009 spells `IC_`), `DEBUG*` macros for the serial output, settings in nvs through `KL_nvs`.

One ESP-IDF project per product (`cam/`, `air/`) on the shared component `components/kibbl_core`: `KL_stream`, `KL_commands`, `KL_wifi_prov`, `KL_config` (server url, device secret, local key), `KL_ota`, `KL_nvs`, `KL_serial`, `KL_time`. The core calls the product through `KL_product.h` (info, its commands, telemetry, the stream work, what stops for an update, factory reset) and shows its status through `KL_status_led.h`; each product implements both in its `main` (linked as `WHOLE_ARCHIVE`, nothing in `main` calls the hooks itself).

### kibbl. air (`air/`): the Conny CO2 traffic light

ISIS IC EHW0001-0013 with a new firmware (the original ESW0002 is backed up in `(40) Conny/backup`). ESP32-C3; the SCD30 and an io expander (PCA9554 or PCF8574A, found at boot) on one I2C bus (IO4/IO5, 100 kHz); the leds (red, green, amber, neutral white, on/off through constant current drivers) and the WDI of the external watchdog MAX6371 are expander pins. Everything below runs without the network; events wait for the server.

| Module          | Task |
| --------------- | ---- |
| `main.c`        | boot order: the watchdog feed first, then the measurement, then the network |
| `KL_product`    | the core's hooks: state, commands (`air_config`, `fan`, `lamp`, `blink`, `night`, `calibrate`), telemetry, events |
| `KL_air`        | the measurement task: SCD30 every 2 s, the mean of 3 for the traffic light with 50 ppm hysteresis, buzzer, fan auto, the detectors, forced recalibration (the detectors pause during it and 15 min after, only the fixed fire temperature stays), night mode; temperature corrected for the warmth of the board (ESW0002's measurements: 4-12 °C by leds and fan) |
| `KL_smoke`      | cigarettes from CO2 puffs of exhaled smoke (a short spike above a slow baseline, back within 90 s; 3 puffs within 4 min); the button logs one by hand, the event says whether a press belongs to it | While a window is open puffs are lower and shorter (diluted, carried out): 40 % of the puff height (at least 40 ppm) and 2 puffs make a cigarette, marked as smoked at the window; the window closes by CO2 only when it stays up 1.5 min, not on a puff.
| `KL_fire`       | a possible fire (CO2 and heat rising together, heat rising fast, or 60 °C at the sensor): red flashing and the alarm pattern of smoke alarms, also at night; the button silences it, 5 calm minutes end it |
| `KL_window`     | an open window: 30 s means over 3 min, the CO2 falling towards 420 ppm with the temperature, or steeply alone, or the temperature 0.8 °C alone while the CO2 does not rise (not for 15 min after the lamp or the fan changed); closed when either rises | The calibration at the open window records the home's fresh air (lowest and highest CO2 of its last 2 minutes, calibrated): falling into that range counts as open, clearly above it as closed; right after a restart (not after a calibration) being in it counts too, without an alert. A steep fall of the raw readings (trend over 60 s, at least 8 ppm a minute or 6 % of the excess over fresh air, 15 ppm in all, from a level that was not lower before: no cigarette puff) counts within about a minute; a window seen opening plays a short tone.
| `KL_events`     | the events queue (32), sent in order with their age once the server is reached |
| `KL_scd30`      | Sensirion SCD30 over I2C with crc, settings written only when they differ (they live in its flash) |
| `KL_ioexp`      | the expander, the power-on flag (it keeps its state over an ESP reset), the watchdog feed task (1 s) |
| `KL_lamp`       | the traffic light task: blinks, provisioning and errors in white, night off, modes traffic/off/white/warm/candle/custom colours; a change fades over 0.6 s by switching fast between the old and the new look; during a firmware update the colours fade round and round |
| `KL_fan`        | pwm 25 kHz: off, on, pulse, auto (the default: 20 % up to 400 ppm below yellow, then rising with the CO2 to the set level at red, 2 % a second), capped at night |
| `KL_buzzer`     | the tones of ESW0002 on a timer, muted at night |
| `KL_button`     | short press: a cigarette logged; held 10 s: the Wi-Fi forgotten |
| `KL_air_config` | thresholds, buzzer, asc, altitude, lamp, fan, smoke, night fan in nvs |

### kibbl. light (`light/`): the Magic Home controller of the TV's RGB strip

A Magic Home ZJ-WFMN-A V1.1 (ESP8285, 1 MB flash, three low side MOSFETs on GPIO 12/5/13) behind the TV, reflashed (`docs/light.md`). ESP-IDF 5 has no ESP8266, and the ESP8266_RTOS_SDK with TLS does not fit two images into 1 MB (about 615 KB each), so this one is an Arduino sketch (`main/main.ino`, esp8266 core 3.1.2, `main/sketch.yaml`) with its own counterparts of the core's modules: the same protocol, commands, pairing code, setup page (`kibbl_core/portal.html`, gzipped) and conventions. The image is about 490 KB: two fit, which updates need. Everything below runs from the loop, without tasks.

| Module          | Task |
| --------------- | ---- |
| `main.ino`      | boot order: the crash count, the settings, the light of before the power cut, the network |
| `KL_product`    | the hooks: info, `rgb` and `blink`, telemetry, the update; `KIBBL-APP:KIBBL-LIGHT:<version>` in the image for the server (an ESP8266 image has no `esp_app_desc_t`) |
| `KL_strip`      | the strip: one colour, fades as the eye sees them (gamma 2.2, pwm 1 kHz, 1000 steps), blinks, provisioning in blue, the breathing of an update; stored 3 s after it last changed |
| `KL_stream`     | websocket to the server over BearSSL: hello, telemetry, commands, ping |
| `KL_tls`        | the roots of `KL_certs.h` (Let's Encrypt, Google Trust Services, SSL.com; `tools/certs.py`), the time from NTP for their validity |
| `KL_commands`   | the command set of serial and server (`docs/protocol.md`) |
| `KL_wifi`       | station with the network the sdk keeps, the setup portal without one, scan, network change with fallback |
| `KL_portal`     | the setup page on the Wi-Fi `kibbl-xxxx`, a minimal http server and a name server for every name |
| `KL_ota`        | the image next to the running one, copied over it at the restart by the loader of the core |
| `KL_recovery`   | 3 crashes in a row before the server was reached: safe mode, only the network and updates (there is no image to go back to) |
| `KL_config`     | the secret, pairing code, server, debug and the light in one EEPROM sector with a crc |

Memory: frames live in PSRAM (4 MB on the ESP32-CAM); the driver keeps two UXGA-sized jpeg buffers, the hub and each consumer one copy that grows with the frame.

## Server (`backend/`, TypeScript, Express)

One process on purpose: the device sockets, the stream viewers and the motion state live in its memory.

* `sockets/device-socket.ts`: authenticates the upgrade (trust on first use), routes json and frames.
* `core/devices.ts`: who is connected, commands with responses, and the **stream demand**: live rate while viewers or snapshot requests exist, motion rate while motion detection is on, else 0. The camera sends nothing nobody needs.
* `core/frames.ts`: latest frame per camera, the mjpeg responses (`multipart/x-mixed-replace`, any `<img>` plays it), slow viewers skip frames instead of falling behind.
* `core/motion.ts` + `motionDetector.ts`: every analysed frame is shrunk to 64×48 grey and compared to a learned background. Changed share above the sensitivity threshold in two frames in a row starts an event; 10 s of calm ends it. Light switched on or a changed exposure (mean jump, more than 55 % changed) resets the background instead. Only when the camera's `motion.keepPictures` is on (off by default) is a picture kept: the frame with the most change. Otherwise the event is activity only (start, end, how much moved) and no frame is written; switched on during an event, that event takes the next frame. Pictures already kept stay when it is switched off, until deleted in the app (`deleteEventPictures`, the events stay) or by retention. With `motion.catOnly` (off by default, good for the feeder) a movement becomes an event only once the cat detector (`catDetector.ts`, YOLOX on the server, throttled to one check a second) finds a cat in one of its frames; until then it stays unconfirmed and is dropped when it ends. Without a detection model the check never blocks, so every movement is activity as before. A change of the whole picture's brightness (auto exposure, clouds) is subtracted before pixels are compared. A new motion event flashes the camera's white light once (`blink`); the 2 s after a flash are not analysed and the next frame becomes the background, so the flash and the settling exposure never count as motion.
* `core/storage.ts`: kept pictures on disk by camera and day, deleted with their events after `RETENTION_DAYS`.
* `health/`: the data of a fitness tracker (a Fitbit Air) through the **Google Health API v4**, ported from whoofit, and **kept on this server**. `google.ts` signs in with OAuth 2.0 + PKCE, refreshes tokens and waits when Google rate-limits (429). `tokens.ts` keeps the tokens encrypted (AES-256-GCM, key from `HEALTH_TOKEN_KEY` or `JWT_SECRET`) in `healthlinks`. `history.ts` fetches everything once, newest first: every data type in 30-day windows back until 90 days in a row bring nothing (heart rate zones do not count: Google computes them for any day), then the heart rate of every complete day at full resolution; resumable. After that only the latest data: each sync of the tracker re-reads the last 2 days (Google revises them, sleep is reprocessed), and a day's heart rate is stored once the tracker synced after it ended. `store.ts`: the data points as Google sent them in `healthpoints` by type and civil day, a fetched window replacing what was stored for its days (idempotent, never doubled); heart rate as binary per day in `heartratedays` (~300 kB a day). `extract.ts` turns points into series (whoofit's parsers, identical output, Python rounding included), `service.ts` builds the page's summary from the stored points for 30 days, 90 days, a year or all, cached until the next change. Data types that start to return data are found every 30 minutes, fetched back once and stored from then on.
* `gym/`: training of every kind in one place (lifting, running, cycling, Pilates, yoga and mobility, HIIT, swimming, walking, sport), so the week counts everything that was done, not only what one app knows of. `shared/gym.ts` holds the model and the analysis for the server and the page: the muscles (21, each in a group: chest, back, shoulders, biceps, triceps, forearms, core, quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves) with the hard sets a week that grow a group (about 10-20 for the large ones, Schoenfeld 2017 and Pelland 2024, less for the small ones), a catalog of about 270 exercises named as Strong names them (primary and secondary muscles, equipment, kind: weight, bodyweight, assisted, a hold, mobility, cardio), a guess of the muscles from the words of an unknown name, workout kinds, starter templates (a weights split and a run, intervals, a ride, Pilates on the mat and the reformer, a mobility flow, HIIT). Sets count 1 for a muscle trained first, ½ for one that helps, a group an exercise's sets once; stretches and cardio count no hard sets. The estimated 1RM is Epley's (reps past 12 count as 12); the next session follows double progression (reps up within the range, then +2.5 kg, +5 kg for the large lower body lifts); the advice: groups below or far above their range, groups trained on one day a week, exercises without a better set in their last 3 of 6 weeks, pushing against pulling, hamstrings against quads, rear delts, and what improved. `service.ts`: workouts in `gymworkouts` (one document a workout with its exercises and sets; `end` null while it runs, one at a time), `gymtemplates` (the starters once for a new account), `gymexercises` (the user's own, also those an import met, muscles guessed), the profile in the user (`gym.sex` for the drawings, `gym.bodyweight`). The fitness tracker's sessions (Google Health's `exercise` points of `health/`, records of one session by two sources made one, under 5 minutes and chores left out) join a logged workout they overlap (heart rate, calories, zone minutes) or stand as a workout of their own (`source: 'tracker'`, made when read, never stored). `strong.ts` reads the CSV of Strong's export (Settings, Export Strong Data; both its layouts, columns by name, warm-up/drop/failure sets, rest timer rows, lbs and miles converted): an import replaces the workouts of an earlier import at the same start, never doubles them; `learnTemplates` makes a template of each workout name done twice or more (its latest session), replacing the starter weights split and templates of that name. Strong's new export has no unit column: it writes each exercise in the unit set for it, so the import reads the file first (`/api/gym/import/strong/preview`): each exercise with its weights and the unit the file proves (a set logged before the unit changed comes out odd: 25 kg as 55.12 lbs, 40 lb as 18.14 kg), the person sets the rest, and each exercise keeps its unit (`gymexercises.unit`): the app shows and takes its weights in it, they are stored in kg. A decimal point missed (1254 for 125.4, about ten times the exercise's usual weight, its tenth usual) is corrected at the import, Strong copies it from session to session; a set above 3× an exercise's median never becomes a record. `suggestTemplates` (`/api/gym/suggestions`): the routine as it is done (each workout name done 3 times or more in 12 weeks: the exercises of half its last 8 sessions in their usual order, their usual sets and reps, how often a week), the hard sets a week it gives each group, then: a group far above its range keeps the exercises done most on the day for it (4 chest or back, 3 shoulders, 2 others; a day is for the groups of its name, "Chest/Tri") and 1 on another day, then sets down to 3; the legs get a day of their own when they hardly have sets (their exercises move there, 2 quads, 2 hamstrings, 1 glutes, 1 calves); a group below its range gets sets (up to 4, 5 on a new day), then an exercise on a day that trains what goes with it; rear delts against the pressing; a stalled lift (5 sessions in 6 weeks, the last 4 no better) gets the other rep range, an isolation lift never below 8 reps. Each change says why; the page saves the ones taken. `buildWorkout`: a one-time workout for muscles tapped on the figures, the exercises done most for each (a compound lift first), as they were usually done; at the end of a workout not started from a template it can be kept as one. Each set ticked keeps its time (`at`): the rests taken between sets, and when each exercise ran (from the tick before its first set to its last); a workout can be paused (`pausedAt`, `pausedMs`, left out of its duration). The heart rate of a workout (`/api/gym/workouts/:id/heartrate`, a tracker session too) comes from the tracker's stored heart rate (`health/`; days without it are never asked of Google): the curve, mean, peak, minutes per zone, and each exercise's mean and peak over its time (estimated for an import, which has no times of the sets: the workout's time shared out by its sets); an exercise's over its sessions `/api/gym/exercises/:name/heartrate`. Sessions the tracker found by itself (Google Health's `recordingMethod` PASSIVELY_MEASURED: walks, chores) are shown, muted, never counted as workouts; started on the tracker (ACTIVELY_MEASURED) or entered (MANUAL) they count. Favorite exercises (`gym.favorites`): first in the lists, preferred by the builder, kept and preferred by the suggestions, an exercise started on its own from its sheet. A template is started (also at one of its exercises), viewed (its exercises with their last three times) or its progress shown (each exercise's chart and change of 1RM). An exercise's last three times are compared set by set (set 1 with set 1...: weight × reps, a bar for its estimated 1RM, the newest against the one before), with what it says: reps falling at the same weight (hold it or lower it). An exercise's picture opens its sheet; from there it is added to the workout that runs, or starts one. The muscles of the Progress tab follow the weeks chosen (1, 4, 12, 26, 52). Api `/api/gym/*`, MCP `kibbl_gym_workouts` (with `heart_rate`), `kibbl_gym_progress` and `kibbl_gym_suggestions`.
* `meters/`: meters of the **EMONI LoRaWAN server** (ttn-chirpstack-webhook, container `emoni-lorawan` on the same host), **copied to this server**. `source.ts` reads its api (`METER_SOURCE_URL`) over its docker network. `service.ts` copies everything once (every reading with meter value, battery voltage and RSSI; consumption per day, month and year in Europe/Berlin, the months and years back to the manual readings before the meter reported), then, each time the source announces a reading on its live stream (server-sent events, `/events`), the readings after the newest stored one and the latest 3 days, 2 months and 2 years again (every reconnect catches up with all meters), and tells the open pages (`meterUpdate`); upserts on unique indexes, never doubled (`meters`, `meterreadings`, `meterperiods`). The state: latest reading, power over the last 2 hours (a meter counting in 0.1 kWh steps repeats its value), today, the battery in % between the source's full and empty voltage, its drain per day in the current cycle and the days left.
* `core/printer.ts`: the 3D printer a kibbl. cam reaches (`docs/printer.md`): its state checked field by field, shown at once when the print changes (not for the temperatures), and an alert to the owner when a print finishes or fails.
* `core/air.ts`: the kibbl. air. Its telemetry (every 10 s) is stored as a mean per minute (`airreadings`, unique by device and minute); its events (`smoke`, `window`, `fire`) are dated by their `ageSeconds` and kept in `airevents`; cigarettes per day count every press of the button plus the detected ones no press belongs to. Night mode by schedule: every telemetry says whether it is on, the server sends `night` when that is not what the schedule says, a switch in the app holds until the schedule changes next. A health hint of the climate (`shared/climate.ts`) that lasts 30 min goes to the owner, the same one at most every 12 h, never in night mode; a window opened and a fire at once.
* `shared/climate.ts`: the room climate and health: temperature (WHO 18 °C), humidity (40-60 %), CO2 (German Environment Agency), mould at the inner surface of outer walls (estimated with the outside temperature and fRsi 0.7 of DIN 4108-2; 80 % there lets mould grow), what airing does now (water in g/m³ inside and outside). Shared by the app and the alerts.
* `core/weather.ts`: temperature and humidity outside the home from Open-Meteo, kept 15 min per place: the home location each account chooses in Settings (searched with Open-Meteo's geocoding, or the browser's location; rounded to about 1 km), else `HOME_LATITUDE`/`HOME_LONGITUDE` of the server.
* `analysis/`: everything that stands out across all parts, for the Analysis page (`/analysis`, `/api/analysis`, `?fresh=1`) and MCP `kibbl_analysis`. Each part analysed on its own (a failing one shows its error): heat (the insights of `heat/`), air (each online kibbl. air: the advice of its room, sensor error, fire, stale air last night in a bedroom, cigarettes in 7 days, its thermometer against tado's in the room over 6 h, 2 °C info, 4 °C warn), heating (tado read errors, budget, no read for 2 h, open windows), meters (sync, no reading for 6 h, battery under 60 days, yesterday 1.4× or 0.6× the median day of 4 weeks, the base load 01:00 to 05:00), devices (offline 15 min, camera error, updates, approvals) and health (sync, sign-in ending, tracker battery, resting heart rate, HRV and sleep of the last 7 days against the 28 before). `checks.ts` holds the pure checks. A report is kept 2 minutes per account.
* `heat/`: the heat used in the home from readings entered by hand, from a Techem report or by an assistant (`kibbl_heat_readings_add`): heat cost allocators on radiators (units × the radiator's factor) and meters of the whole home (kWh, MWh, m³), collections `heatmeters` and `heatreadings` (one value per meter and day, a running total; a lower one is a reset). `estimate.ts` spreads the use between two readings over its days: by tado's heating hours (mean heating power × 24 h) of the tado rooms in the meter's room when they cover every day, else by degree days G20/15 from Open-Meteo's archive at the home location (`outside.ts`), else evenly. Heating cost reports (Techem's Heizkostenabrechnung, scans) are uploaded as PDF, kept under `REPORT_DATA_PATH` (default next to the images, `/data/reports`), read on the server with poppler's `pdftoppm` and Tesseract (`ocr.ts`, German, one report at a time, nothing leaves the server) and parsed in `report.ts` (devices with room code and readings, period, the building's gas kWh and units); the person checks the values against the PDF and imports them (`reports.ts`): a heat meter per allocator, found again by its number, readings the day before the period and on the reading day, factor = use / difference of the readings. The latest imported report's kWh per unit (building kWh / building units) shows the radiators' units in kWh. `insights.ts` with `analysis.ts`: the radiators ranked by their use over the last year of readings, and hints where heat is wasted: a radiator needing 1.4× its usual units per degree day or counting through warm weeks (valve), tado heating while a kibbl. air in the room saw an open window (from the closing events and their open seconds) or windows open for hours on cold days, tado at 60 % or more while the room stays 1 °C below target (bleeding), a room cooling 1.5× faster at night than the others (night temperatures of the kibbl. air over the difference to outside, nights with an open window or tado heating left out); cached 10 minutes per account (`/api/heat/insights`, MCP `kibbl_heat_insights`). The same upload may hold the landlord's statement of the running costs (Betriebs-/Nebenkostenabrechnung): `statement.ts` reads its table (each cost with the building's total, how it is shared and the home's share, between Abrechnungsposten and Summe gesamt), the advance paid, the balance and from the letter the new advance and its start; a statement alone needs no import and its breakdown shows on the page. `costs.ts` (`/api/heat/costs`, the user's `heatCosts`): the monthly advance for running costs and heating (with the previous one and the month it changed), the other running costs of the last statement, the heating's base share and price per unit (both default to the latest report's: costs − price × units, Verbrauchskosten; the advance and the other costs default to the latest statement's); `heatBudget` in `shared/heat.ts` gives the year's advance minus other costs and base share as the units it pays for, the page shows this year's use priced and the room left. Page `/heat`.
* `tado/`: heating through tado's REST API (not public; as tado's app and PyTado use it). `client.ts`: the device code sign-in (the person confirms a code at tado, the password stays there), tokens encrypted (AES-256-GCM, key from `HEALTH_TOKEN_KEY` or `JWT_SECRET`), refresh tokens single use and stored before use, every request read for tado's `ratelimit` header (100 a day without Auto-Assist). `service.ts`: all rooms in one request every 2 min with 300 or more requests left, 5 min with 150, else 20 min (`zoneStates` on my.tado.com, `rooms` on hops.tado.com for tado X), every 60 min below 20 requests left, only commands below 8; readings kept per room (`tadoreadings`); commands (a temperature until the next schedule change, by hand or for minutes; off; back to the schedule) as one request each; a kibbl. air's open window switches a heating room in its room off for at most 30 min, back to the schedule when it closes. `pid.ts`: the adaptive PID control (app: Adaptive on a room's card, MCP `kibbl_heating_set` mode `adaptive`), an outer loop over tado's own: after each read (at most every 10 min) the room's temperature against the wish sets tado above or below it (−3 to +5 °C, 5–25 °C, steps of 0.5 with hysteresis, each setting for 2 h and renewed so tado falls back to its schedule if the server stops; changes at least 10 min apart, 30 below 150 requests left, none below 20). The room is learned as dT/dt = a·(u − T) + b by recursive least squares with forgetting (0.97), τ = 1/a (20–240 min) tunes the gains by IMC (λ 60 min, dead time 15 min: Kp = τ/(λ+θ), Ti = τ, Td = θ/2), derivative on the measurement, integral with anti-windup; kept in `tadolinks.auto` across restarts. An open window (tado's detection or a kibbl. air in the room) switches the room off (at least 10 min, the integral held) until it closes; any other command for the room, or a setting changed in tado's own app, ends the control.
* `core/rooms.ts`: the rooms of an account's home (`rooms`). Devices and tado's rooms stand in them (made from tado's room names when it connects); a kibbl. air in the same room as a tado room shows tado's temperature and humidity (`roomClimate` in shared/protocol.ts), its CO2 stays its own. Moving a device is choosing another room (Devices, the Air page).
* `core/notify.ts`: alerts to a device's owner as ntfy push messages with `NTFY_URL` (a fire with the highest priority), by e-mail only a fire and a window open for too long (15 minutes below 10 °C outside or with no weather, 30 minutes up to 18 °C, never above).
* `sockets/browser-socket.ts`: the app's api over socket.io, cookie-authenticated; device commands are whitelisted by capability and field-checked.

## App (`frontend/`, Vite, Tabler)

Multi-page like the enny-cloud-app-service: `src/pages/*.html` with the navbar partial injected at build time, one script per page, built into `backend/dist/frontend`.

| Page          | Content |
| ------------- | ------- |
| `/`           | cameras with their latest picture, next to them each meter with its last 14 days and the resting heart rate of 30 days, today's activity by hour, latest events, controls of non-camera devices |
